🏠
High severity
#01
Hero section lacks hierarchy clarity
The headline "Truckers Wanted" is bold but the value proposition beneath is a dense paragraph with no scannable structure. A first-time visitor cannot tell within 5 seconds whether this is free, paid, or subscription. The "Buy" and "Download Demo" CTAs compete equally with no visual weight distinction.
🎠
High severity
#02
Carousel without controls
The homepage carousel autoplays with no pause controls, visible dots or progress indicator, and no pre-loaded thumbnails. A new user can't see how to stop it. This fails WCAG 2.1 AA Success Criterion 2.2.2 on motion control.
⭐
Strength
#03
Strong social proof placement
Steam Awards and review counts are placed directly below the hero, providing immediate credibility signals. The live "currently playing" player count in the footer reinforces an active community. These are well-executed trust elements that reduce purchase anxiety.
🃏
High severity
#04
Feature cards lack scannable depth
The feature tiles link to anchor points on the About page. However, they are visually indistinct — identical size, no imagery, no micro-copy differences. Users cannot quickly identify which feature matters most to them before clicking through.
📱
High severity
#05
Mobile experience is degraded
Navigation collapses appropriately but critical elements like the DLC expansion amount only become very small without legible labels. The hero paragraph text requires horizontal scrolling on narrow viewports. No mobile-specific affordances are provided for the truck carousel or feature sections.

High severity
#06
Country descriptions are repetitive and exhausting
The "Explore Europe" section lists DLC countries as an extremely long scroll with information density nearly identical for each. An interactive grid would deliver the same information in a fraction of the space.
🚛
Strength
#07
Interactive truck viewer is a standout
The 360° truck configurator widget on the About page is the site's strongest interactive element. Users can rotate trucks and toggle customisation categories, directly communicating the game's depth. This is excellent engagement design for the core audience.
🧭
Medium severity
#08
No contextual buy nudges during exploration
The About page is rich with content but contains very few contextual CTAs. A user who becomes excited reading about the career system or company management has to scroll all the way to the nav or footer to convert. In-context nudges are entirely absent.
👥
High severity
#09
Modding section undersells the community
The modding section is text-heavy with category labels but no screenshots, links to popular mods, or community size numbers. For a game with an enormous modding ecosystem, this section punishes interest by delivering a long name-drop differentiation.

High severity
#10
No prices displayed anywhere
Clicking any DLC sends the user to Steam for pricing. This is a major conversion friction point — users cannot compare value or make purchase decisions without leaving the site. Even approximate "from $X" pricing would dramatically reduce drop-off.
🛒
High severity
#11
Wishlist vs Buy CTAs are visually identical
Recurring DLC pages use "Wishlist" and "Buy on Steam" in an identical Pinterest-style CTA. On a warning page there are no visual signals that some products are not yet purchasable, leading to friction and mismatched expectations.
🗺️
Medium severity
#12
DLC list is overwhelming: no discovery aid
13+ DLC packs are listed with no filtering, no sorting by region or popularity, and no recommended entry point for new players. The cognitive load is severe for any first-time buyer trying to decide where to spend money.
🖼️
Strength
#13
Screenshot thumbnails per DLC are helpful
Each DLC page includes 3 thumbnail screenshots giving users a regional preview of each expansion's visual style before purchase. This is a solid content decision that meaningfully aids visual decision-making and communicates the visual breadth of each DLC pack.
User Journey — First-time visitor to purchase
Discovery
User arrives from Google search or YouTube. Lands on the homepage. "Truckers Wanted" is bold and on-brand but the wall of descriptive text below requires effort to parse. No immediate price or clear value summary visible.
Mixed
Credibility check
Steam Awards and review quote quickly build credibility. The live player count (~10K currently playing) provides strong social proof. The feature grid gives a top-level view of game depth, creating confidence in overall offering.
Strong
Research — About
The 360° truck viewer is impressive but the 31-country scroll is exhausting. They want to understand scope but the UI buries the map. The repetitive country descriptions make the user skim and miss key features.
Friction
Purchase intent
User clicks Buy and immediately faces 13+ DLC options with no prices and no "start here" recommendation. The base game is at the top but the value of each expansion isn't immediately clear. Users who haven't already decided face a confusing wall of choices.
High drop
Conversion
User clicks "Buy on Steam" and is redirected to Steam. The Steam context is understandable given platform constraints, but all conversion data and abandoned-cart recovery happens outside SCS Software's control.
Dependent
Strengths & Weaknesses
✓ What works well
  • Brand atmosphere is strong — cinematic visuals match the game genre
  • Truck configurator is polished and immediately communicates depth
  • Steam Awards + review pull quote build instant credibility
  • DLC screenshots (landscape + interior) are polished and well-designed
  • Community ties (34k AER subscribers) is excellent social differentiation
  • Live player counter bridges the site to the living game community
✗ What needs improvement
  • No prices on the Buy page — a major conversion blocker
  • DLC discovery has no filtering, sorting, or new-user recommendation
  • Autoplay carousel with no controls — fails accessibility standards
  • Wishlist and Buy CTAs look identical — mismatched user expectations
  • About page country list is repetitive and excessively long
  • Mobile experience degrades significantly — small text, broken layouts
Recommendations
01
Add price architecture to the Buy page
Add "Buy from $X on Steam" alongside each DLC listing. Even a relative indicator ($/$$/$$$ ) would reduce decision friction and prevent users from having to leave the site to evaluate value before returning to purchase.
Critical
02
Introduce a DLC recommendation flow
Add a short two-question selector at the top of the Buy page. "New to ETS2? Get the base game. Like Scandinavia? Try Nordic Horizons." A short preference quiz in a region filter would dramatically reduce cognitive load for new players.
Critical
03
Add carousel controls for accessibility
The autorotating carousel needs pause, previous, and next controls. This is both a usability issue (users can't examine screenshots at their own pace) and an accessibility failure under WCAG 2.1 AA Success Criterion 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide).
High
04
Replace the country list with an interactive map
The wall of text on the About page should be replaced with a clickable SVG map of Europe. Users can click each EU country or region to expand its description inline. This would visually communicate the game's scale and improve browsing experience significantly.
High
05
Differentiate primary and secondary CTAs visually
The "Wishlist" CTA should be outlined or secondary. On the Buy page, "Wishlist" should use a bookmark icon and softer styling (outlined muted button) to clearly signal it is not yet purchasable content.
High
06
Add in-context purchase nudges on the About page
After each major feature section (Trucks, Career, Company Management) add a small contextual CTA like "Ready to build your company? Get ETS2 →". This captures purchase intent at peak engagement rather than forcing a navigation detour at the wrong moment.
Medium
